A wedding timeline that holds up

City Garden, Varanasi  ·  6 November 2026  ·  7 min read

Most wedding timelines you find online are written for somebody else's wedding. They have eighteen months in them, a florist consultation and a cake tasting. A wedding in Varanasi is usually arranged in a tighter window, by a family rather than a planner, and around dates that a pandit gives you rather than ones you pick.

So here is a shorter, more honest version. The principle behind it: only four or five things in a wedding are genuinely date-locked. Get those right early and the rest has far more give in it than it feels like it does at the time.

Eight to twelve months out — the four locks

If you only do four things this early, do these.

  • The date. Everything else is downstream of it. Get the shubh muhurat settled before you shortlist anything.
  • The venue. Peak dates in Varanasi — the November to February stretch and the Dev Uthani window especially — go eight to ten months ahead. This is the single booking that cannot be solved with money later.
  • The guest number. A rough one is fine. Three hundred and eight hundred are different weddings, and almost every later decision needs this figure.
  • The budget shape. Not a spreadsheet. Just the total and roughly how it splits between venue, catering, decor and everything else.

Catering is often tied to the venue, so ask at this stage whether the venue has its own kitchen and whether outside caterers are allowed. The answer changes your plan more than you would think.

Five to six months out

Now the things that need lead time but not panic.

  • Photographer and videographer — the good ones in Varanasi book out for the season early.
  • Rooms for out-of-town guests. If you expect forty people flying or training in, this is a real logistics item, not an afterthought.
  • Outfits that need stitching, and anything being made to order.
  • The shape of the functions: how many events, which day, what time, where.

Three months out

This is when the wedding stops being an idea.

  • Invitations designed and printed. Digital first if the list is long; print for the people who would be hurt not to receive one.
  • Decor brief agreed with the venue or the decorator. Colours, mandap style, lighting, stage.
  • Menu finalised, with the actual tasting done. Do not skip the tasting.
  • Sangeet music, DJ or band confirmed.
  • Mehendi artist, makeup artist, pandit — all booked, all with a time written down.

One month out

  • Guest count confirmed to the caterer, with the honest number rather than the hopeful one.
  • A written run-sheet: every function, its start time, who is responsible.
  • Transport for the baraat and for guests between hotel and venue.
  • Payments scheduled so nobody is arranging a transfer on the wedding morning.
  • A visit to the venue to physically walk the layout: where the baraat enters, where the mandap sits, where dinner goes, where people park.

The last week

Nothing new gets added in the last week. That is the whole rule. The last week is for confirming, not deciding.

  • Call every vendor once. Confirm time, place and contact number.
  • Give one person — a cousin, a friend, not the parents — the run-sheet and the vendor list. They are the phone number everyone rings on the day.
  • Pack a box: outfits, jewellery, documents, chargers, safety pins, a change of clothes, medicine. It sounds trivial until it isn't.
  • Check the weather and decide, in advance, what the rain plan is.

A timeline is not there to make the wedding perfect. It is there so that the morning of the wedding has nothing in it that you have to think about.

What to do if you have far less time

Plenty of weddings in Varanasi are put together in eight or ten weeks, and they are fine. If that is your situation, compress in this order: lock the venue and the caterer on day one, the photographer on day two, and let the invitations be digital. Decor and outfits can move fast. Dates and venues cannot.
